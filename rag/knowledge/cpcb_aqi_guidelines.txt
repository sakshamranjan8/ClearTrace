CPCB NATIONAL AIR QUALITY INDEX (NAQI) GUIDELINES
===================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) of India developed the National Air Quality Index (NAQI) to communicate air quality status to the public in a simple, uniform format. The AQI transforms complex pollutant concentration data into a single number on a scale of 0 to 500. Higher AQI values indicate worse air quality and greater health concern.

AQI CATEGORIES AND BREAKPOINTS
-------------------------------
The CPCB NAQI defines six categories:

Good (0-50): Air quality is satisfactory and poses little or no health risk. Minimal impact on health for all population groups.

Satisfactory (51-100): Air quality is acceptable. Minor breathing discomfort may occur in sensitive individuals after prolonged outdoor exposure.

Moderate (101-200): Breathing discomfort may occur in people with lung disease, asthma, heart disease, children, and older adults. General public is less likely to be affected.

Poor (201-300): Breathing discomfort on prolonged exposure for most people. People with heart or lung disease, children, and older adults should limit prolonged outdoor exertion.

Very Poor (301-400): Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure. Significant aggravation of symptoms in people with lung and heart disease. Widespread impacts across the general population.

Severe (401-500): Serious health impacts even on healthy people. Emergency conditions with respiratory effects on large sections of the population. Avoid all outdoor physical activity.

AQI CALCULATION METHOD
-----------------------
The overall AQI is determined using the maximum sub-index method. A sub-index is calculated for each monitored pollutant by linear interpolation within the appropriate breakpoint range. The highest sub-index among all pollutants becomes the reported AQI value for that station.

Sub-index formula: Ip = ((IHI - ILO) / (BHI - BLO)) * (Cp - BLO) + ILO
Where Ip is the sub-index, Cp is the pollutant concentration, BHI and BLO are breakpoint concentrations, and IHI and ILO are the corresponding AQI breakpoint values.

Averaging periods: PM2.5, PM10, NO2, and SO2 use 24-hour rolling averages. CO and O3 use 8-hour rolling averages.

CPCB requires a minimum of 3 sub-indices to compute a valid AQI, and at least one of those must be a particulate matter sub-index (PM2.5 or PM10). If fewer than 3 sub-indices are available, the AQI is reported as insufficient data.

The pollutant responsible for the highest sub-index is identified as the dominant or predominant pollutant for that monitoring period.
